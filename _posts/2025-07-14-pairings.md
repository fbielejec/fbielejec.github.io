# <a name="intro"/> Introduction

In this post I will go over ELlliptic Curve pairings.
Pairing are often sold as the "moon math" component of the ZeroKnowledge protocols (or at least for the ones based on pairings).

What follows is that they are often treated as a black-box, simply because covering them in a sufficient detail would take a considerable amount of time, and in most cases is not strictly needed from the engineering perspective.

I this blog post will explain what the black box does, _alas_ with the engineer's perspective in mind, therefore keeping the mathematical details down to a required minimum and with numerical examples to help guide the understanding of how pairings are constructed.

Note that there is a substantial amount of subject matter knowledge assumed, a broad but not exhaustive list can be found in the [Prerequisites](#pre) section below.

# <a name="why"/> Why

Let $\mathbb{F}_p$ be a finite field of a characteristic $p$ (a prime number) and let $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ be an elliptic curve.
Points on $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$, together with the so called abstractly defined point-at-infinity $\mathcal{O}$, which serves as the identity element, form an additive, cyclic group, with the group law being the elliptic curve point addition.

We have fast and efficient algorithms for point addition, as well as point-doubling (adding a point ot iotself), and there exists a class of elliptic curve, the so-called [Edwards curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_curve) where there is a closed-form solution for both adding two distinct points as well as point-doubling.

Repeated point addition leads to the notion of the Elliptic Curve scalar multiplication $[\cdot]$:

$[\cdot]G: \med Z_{n} \rightarrow E(\mathbb{F_p}); \med x \mapsto [x]G$

where:

- $E(\mathbb{F})$ is an elliptic curve of order $n$
- $G$ is Generator of $E(\mathbb{F})$
- $[x]G=\underbrace{G + G \cdot \dots \cdot G}_{x \text{ times}}$
- $[0]G=\mathcal{O}$
